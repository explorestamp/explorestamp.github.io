> **Taipei · 8 places**
> His chips are hard to get. The pork knuckle he ate just takes a bit of queuing.

Every May or June, Taipei gets an unusual weather system: the leather-jacket front.

A man in a black leather jacket lands in Taiwan, and for the next few days he takes over both the business news and the food news. In the morning he's on stage talking about the future of AI. By night he's standing at a night-market stall holding a grilled corn cob. The next day, that stall's queue is one street corner longer.

The man is Jensen Huang, founder of NVIDIA. His GPUs come with months-long waiting lists, but the places he's eaten at are open today. This route gathers the restaurants and stalls reported in the news. Eight places and eight stamps let you follow the AI leather-jacket guy's appetite around Taipei in two days.

## Let's be clear

This is a fan-made food map we put together from news reports. It has nothing to do with Jensen Huang himself or with NVIDIA, and he hasn't endorsed it. Every place is listed in the reports at the end of this post.

Also, the leather jacket is optional. Wearing one in a Taipei night-market queue in May takes real faith.

Most of the five restaurants are around Nanjing East Road and Bade Road, Niu Er is in Neihu, and the three night markets are in three different corners of the city. Restaurants by day, night markets by night: two days is just right. Pacing your stomach is up to you.

## Fu Ba Wang Pork Knuckle: even AI can't work out how much to order

In a lane in the Siping Street shopping area is an old pork knuckle shop. According to reports, Jensen Huang came here in October 2023, then came back in May 2025 and ordered nearly every signature dish on the menu.

The specialty is tender braised pork leg over a bowl of rice. If you're on your own, you don't need to copy him and order the whole menu. If there are two of you, two portions of pork knuckle is a perfectly reasonable decision.

Your first stamp starts with this pork knuckle.

## Li Yuan Soup Dumplings: one basket isn't enough, so have another

In a lane off Nanjing East Road, Li Yuan's steamer baskets stack up layer by layer. Reports say he had xiaolongbao and crab roe soup dumplings here.

Eating a soup dumpling takes three steps: pick it up, bite a small hole, blow. Plenty of people fail at step two and only remember step three after burning their tongue. That isn't a computing problem. It's a patience problem.

## Hua Niang Restaurant: the chive stir-fry that made it to the big stage

In a lane by Taipei Arena, Hua Niang's signature dish is "fly's head," a stir-fry of minced pork, chives and fermented black beans. Jensen Huang has been several times and orders it every time. Later, the restaurant's name even appeared on the backdrop of his GTC Taipei keynote, lined up with the logos of tech giants.

A plate of stir-fry making it onto the stage of an AI conference is probably the highest honor in Taiwanese home cooking. We suggest two bowls of rice: one to eat, one as a mark of respect.

## Wang Ji Fucheng Rice Dumplings: the man who took out 14

Jensen Huang was born in Tainan, so his zongzi have to be the southern kind. Wang Ji on Bade Road sells exactly that. Reports say he bought 14 at once in June 2024, and his family came back for three big bags in 2025.

Order a pork zongzi with a bowl of fish ball soup. There's also rice pudding and vermicelli on the menu. You don't need to buy 14, but taking two home for tomorrow's breakfast is very sensible.

## Niu Er Beef Noodles: the last bowl before leaving Taiwan

In Neihu's residential lanes, Niu Er is a place he's been at least three times. In May 2025, his last meal before leaving Taiwan was a bowl of beef noodles here.

Order a bowl with half tendon, half beef, and take your time. If you finish and feel a little reluctant to leave, congratulations: now you know the feeling.

## Raohe Street Night Market: pepper buns and that corn cob

The first night market is Raohe. Walk in through the gate beside Ciyou Temple, and Fuzhou Shizu Pepper Buns is right at the entrance. Reports say he had a pepper bun here and also a cob from Liu's grilled corn stall, and photos of him holding the corn went around the internet for a good while.

Pepper buns are very hot straight from the oven. Please refer to step three under Li Yuan above.

## Ningxia Night Market: tofu pudding, mangosteen and coming back for more

Ningxia is probably his most visited night market. Reports say he has the oyster omelet at Yuan Huan Bian every time he's in Taiwan. Once, at Douhua Zhuang, he paid for everyone in the queue. In June 2026, he got in the car to leave, then came back for tofu pudding and stood at Zhan Ji's fruit stall eating mangosteen.

One headline described him going in and out of Ningxia three times. Our advice is to eat your fill in one go: oyster omelet, tofu pudding, then fruit, in that order, and collect your stamp here.

## Linjiang Street Night Market: finish with shaved snow ice

The last stop is Linjiang Street Night Market, which some people call Tonghua Street Night Market. Reports say he came here one evening in May 2024 for shaved snow ice at Ice World.

After a whole day of pork knuckle, soup dumplings, zongzi and oyster omelets, a bowl of shaved ice is the perfect finish. Put down your spoon beside your eighth stamp, and the food map is complete.

## Two-day plan

**Day 1: around Nanjing East Road, plus two night markets**

1. Lunch at Fu Ba Wang Pork Knuckle
2. In the afternoon, visit Wang Ji Fucheng Rice Dumplings and take two home for tomorrow's breakfast
3. Raohe Street Night Market in the early evening
4. Move on to Ningxia Night Market later (if you still have the energy)

**Day 2: soup dumplings, chive stir-fry, beef noodles and ice to finish**

1. Breakfast on yesterday's zongzi
2. Lunch at Li Yuan Soup Dumplings
3. Niu Er Beef Noodles in Neihu in the afternoon
4. Dinner at Hua Niang Restaurant for the chive stir-fry
5. Finish with shaved snow ice at Linjiang Street Night Market

If two night markets in one evening is too much, move Ningxia to the second night and Hua Niang to the first day.

## Before you go

- Opening hours, closing days and ticket prices can change, so check each place's official website before you set off. All of these places have had long queues after appearing in the news, and reports say Hua Niang needs booking well ahead, so call first for busy times.
- The five restaurants are easy to reach by metro and on foot. For Niu Er, take the Wenhu Line to Xihu Station. All three night markets are near metro stations.
- Photos of the food are fine, but people in the queue and the shop owners may not want to be in your shot, so ask first.
- This is a food route, not an eating challenge. Know your limits and save some room for the next stop.
